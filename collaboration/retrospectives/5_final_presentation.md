# Final Retrospective: Milestone 5 – Final Presentation
  
**Team:** DataCents (Group 15)  
**Syllabus Alignment:** Final Presentation Event  

---

## 📋 Stop Doing

- Relying solely on WhatsApp for critical project communication
- Submitting large, infrequent pull requests that are difficult to review
- Starting slide preparation without a unified narrative structure
- Underestimating the time needed for rehearsal and technical checks

## 🔁 Continue Doing

- Using SHAP and other interpretability tools to make complex results accessible
- Maintaining a clean, well-documented repository structure
- Engaging in peer feedback exchanges with other groups
- Time-boxed collaboration sessions (2-hour sprints)

## 🚀 Start Doing

- Recording video demos of interactive tools (like the DataCents Dashboard)
- Creating "implementation guides" for future researchers
- Developing a one-page project summary for recruiters and stakeholders
- Sharing individual learning reflections in the final README

## 💡 Lessons Learned

1. **Constraints foster creativity**:
The 6-slide/90-second limitation forced us to distill our message to its essence
2. **Visual storytelling transcends language barriers**:
Our SHAP plots communicated more than tables ever could
3. **Preparation beats talent**:
Groups that rehearsed multiple times delivered more compelling presentations
4. **Documentation is a love letter to your future self**:
Our detailed README will help us revisit this work years later

---

## 🎯 Strategy vs. Reality

### ✅ What worked as expected

- Successful deployment of the DataCents Dashboard on Vercel
- Effective division of presentation sections among team members
- Strong alignment between communication strategy and final artifact
- Positive stakeholder feedback on our risk assessment approach

### ❌ What didn't work

- Initial difficulty condensing 6 months of work into 90 seconds
- Technical challenges during final rehearsal (audio/video issues)
- Underestimated time needed for slide formatting and consistency checks
- Last-minute adjustments to handle presentation platform constraints

### ➕ Added to strategy

- Created presentation "cheat sheets" for each team member
- Developed backup plans for live demo failures
- Added timestamps to rehearsal recordings for focused feedback
- Implemented peer review rotation for slide iterations

### ➖ Removed from strategy

- Detailed appendix slides with technical specifications
- Extended Q&A preparation beyond core topics
- Multiple wardrobe coordination meetings
- Complex animation sequences that risked technical failures

## 🔧 Key Improvements for Future Projects

1. **Rehearsal protocol**: Implement structured rehearsal cycles starting 2 weeks before presentation
2. **Technical safeguards**: Create backup plans for every live demo component
3. **Audience targeting**: Develop audience-specific versions of the presentation (technical vs. non-technical)
4. **Peer review system**: Establish rotating peer review partnerships for iterative feedback
5. **Documentation transition**: Create a handoff document for future teams continuing this research

## 🎉 Final Thoughts

Our journey from problem identification to final presentation has been transformative.
We entered as individuals with technical skills and emerged as a team that can navigate
the complete data science lifecycle. While we faced challenges—from data collection
